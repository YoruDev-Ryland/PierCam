## Fixed

- **With auto-exposure on, a recording could be blinding white for half an hour or more when the roof opened or the session started at sunset**, and then almost black for a while after. Auto-exposure only changes the exposure a little each frame while recording, so a smooth twilight doesn't show steps — but that made coming down from a far-too-long exposure take dozens of frames, and the picture's brightness, which is averaged over many frames to stop flicker, then took a while to recover from all that white.
  - Now, when a recorded frame comes out badly over- or under-exposed, PierCam meters the sky on the spot with a few quick test exposures, which are not recorded, and retakes the frame at the right exposure. The brightness averaging starts afresh with it.
  - From a 15-second exposure on a bright evening sky, that takes a handful of test shots and a few seconds, instead of a long run of white frames.
  - The gentle frame-to-frame changes through twilight are unchanged.

  This only applies with **AUTO-EXPOSURE WHILE RECORDING** switched on. If the roof regularly opens on a very bright sky, lowering the shortest exposure under **AUTO-EXPOSURE** gives it room to darken further.
