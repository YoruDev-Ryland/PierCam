## Fixed

- **Cameras that are not 16:9 were recorded stretched.** The resolution setting was handed straight to the encoder, so a square sensor like the ASI676MC was squashed into 1920×1080 and the night could not be recovered afterwards. The chosen size is now a box the sensor is fitted inside, keeping its own proportions — and the same rule applies when the library shrinks an old night, which would otherwise have squashed it a second time.
- The frame count and size estimate on the Live page kept describing the previous camera: it is now recalculated whenever a camera is connected or disconnected. On a 3552×3552 sensor it had been reporting a night as 2.4 GB of raw frames when the real figure was 14.4 GB.

## Changed

- **The resolution list comes from the camera that is connected**, largest first, and always includes the full sensor — an ASI676MC can now record its whole 3552×3552 frame rather than being limited to 16:9 sizes.
  - A size is chosen by its longest edge and the sensor keeps its shape inside it: 1920 is 1920×1080 on a 16:9 camera, 1920×1920 on a square one, 1920×1284 on a 3:2 one.
  - Sizes larger than the sensor are not offered. Enlarging a frame adds no detail and costs bitrate.
  - Nothing changes for a 1920×1080 camera, which still offers 1920×1080, 1280×720 and 960×540.
- The library's shrink targets are named by longest edge for the same reason, so they fit each night's shape instead of imposing 16:9 on it. An existing setting carries over by its longest edge.

## Upgrading

Run the new installer over 1.5 — your settings and timelapse library are kept. Check the resolution on the Config page once: a setting saved before this release carries over by its longest edge, which on a non-16:9 camera now means a larger frame than it did.

Nights already recorded stretched cannot be fully recovered, but scaling one back to its sensor's shape restores the proportions.
